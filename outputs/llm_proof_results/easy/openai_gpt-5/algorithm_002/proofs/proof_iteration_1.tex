\section*{Algorithm Name}
Randomized Coordinate Gradient Descent with Normalized Steps (RCGD-NS)

\section*{Mathematical Formulation}
Let $f:\R^d\to\R$ be differentiable. Denote $e_i\in\R^d$ the $i$-th coordinate vector, and $\nabla_i f(w) := \langle \nabla f(w), e_i\rangle$ the $i$-th partial derivative. Let $L_i>0$ be the coordinate-wise smoothness constants (defined in the Assumptions section). Let $p=(p_1,\ldots,p_d)$ be a probability distribution over coordinates with $p_i>0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^d p_i=1$.

Given step parameter $\eta\in(0,2)$, the iteration is
\[
w_{t+1} \;=\; w_t - \eta\,\frac{1}{L_{i_t}}\,\nabla_{i_t} f(w_t)\, e_{i_t},
\]
where $i_t\sim p$ is sampled independently at each iteration.

Hyperparameters:
- Sampling probabilities $p_i>0$, $\sum_i p_i=1$.
- Step-size scalar $\eta\in(0,2)$.
- Coordinate smoothness constants $L_i>0$ (used for normalization).

\section*{Pseudocode}
Input: initial point $w_0\in\R^d$, probabilities $p_i>0$, coordinate smoothness $L_i>0$, step-size $\eta\in(0,2)$, number of iterations $T$.

For $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$:
- Sample $i_t\in\{1,\dots,d\}$ with $\mathbb{P}(i_t=i)=p_i$.
- Compute scalar gradient $g_t := \nabla_{i_t} f(w_t)$.
- Update $w_{t+1} := w_t - \eta \frac{g_t}{L_{i_t}} e_{i_t}$.

Return $w_T$.

Termination conditions can alternatively be based on a budget $T$, or a tolerance on $f(w_t)-f_\star$, or $\|\nabla f(w_t)\|$.

\section*{Dry Run Example}
Consider $f(w)=(w-3)^2$ on $\R$ (i.e., $d=1$). Then $\nabla f(w)=2(w-3)$, and the coordinate-wise smoothness constant is $L_1=2$ (since $f''(w)=2$). There is a single coordinate, so $p_1=1$. Take $w_0=0$, $\eta=0.1$. The update simplifies to
\[
w_{t+1}=w_t - \eta \frac{1}{L_1}\nabla f(w_t) = w_t - 0.1\cdot \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2(w_t-3)
= w_t - 0.1(w_t-3) = 0.9w_t + 0.3.
\]
- Iteration 0:
  - $w_0=0$, $\nabla f(w_0)=2(0-3)=-6$.
  - $w_1=0.9\cdot 0 + 0.3 = 0.3$.
  - $f(w_1)=(0.3-3)^2=7.29$.
- Iteration 1:
  - $\nabla f(w_1)=2(0.3-3)=-5.4$.
  - $w_2=0.9\cdot 0.3 + 0.3 = 0.57$.
  - $f(w_2)=(0.57-3)^2=5.9049$.
- Iteration 2:
  - $\nabla f(w_2)=2(0.57-3)=-4.86$.
  - $w_3=0.9\cdot 0.57 + 0.3 = 0.813$.
  - $f(w_3)=(0.813-3)^2=4.783\,\!  \overline{  6 }$ (exactly $(2.187)^2=4.783\,\!  969\dots$; to 4 d.p., $4.7840$).

\section*{Assumptions}
We work under the following conditions.

- Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition: There exists $\mu>0$ such that for all $w\in\R^d$,
\[
\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(w)\|^2 \;\ge\; \mu\bigl(f(w)-f_\star\bigr),
\]
where $f_\star := \inf_{w\in\R^d} f(w)$ and the infimum is attained.

- Coordinate-wise smoothness: For each coordinate $i\in\{1,\dots,d\}$ there exists $L_i>0$ such that for all $w\in\R^d$ and all $t\in\R$,
\[
f(w + t e_i) \;\le\; f(w) + t\,\nabla_i f(w) + \frac{L_i}{2}\,t^2.
\]
Equivalently, for each fixed $i$, the univariate function $\varphi_i(t):=f(w+te_i)$ has $L_i$-Lipschitz derivative: $|\varphi_i'(t)-\varphi_i'(s)|\le L_i|t-s|$.

- Sampling: Coordinates are sampled i.i.d. with probabilities $p_i>0$, $\sum_i p_i=1$, independently of the past given $w_t$.

- Step parameter: $\eta\in(0,2)$ is fixed.

\section*{Main Convergence Theorem}
Let $\rho(\eta,p,L,\mu):=\eta(2-\eta)\,\mu\,\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}$. Then for the iterates of RCGD-NS under the Assumptions above,
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(w_{t})-f_\star\bigr] \;\le\; \bigl(1-\rho(\eta,p,L,\mu)\bigr)^t\,\bigl(f(w_0)-f_\star\bigr),\qquad \forall t\ge 0.
\]
In particular, with importance sampling $p_i = \frac{L_i}{\sum_{j=1}^d L_j}$ one has $\min_i \frac{p_i}{L_i}=\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^d L_j}$ and the rate is
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(w_{t})-f_\star\bigr] \;\le\; \left(1-\frac{\eta(2-\eta)\mu}{\sum_{j=1}^d L_j}\right)^t\,\bigl(f(w_0)-f_\star\bigr).
\]
With uniform sampling $p_i=\tfrac{1}{d}$, one has $\min_i \frac{p_i}{L_i}=\frac{1}{d\max_i L_i}$ and
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(w_{t})-f_\star\bigr] \;\le\; \left(1-\frac{\eta(2-\eta)\mu}{d\max_i L_i}\right)^t\,\bigl(f(w_0)-f_\star\bigr).
\]

\section*{Theoretical Properties}
- Linear convergence under PL: The method contracts the expected suboptimality at a geometric rate determined by $\mu$ and the sampling/smoothness profile via $\min_i \frac{p_i}{L_i}$.
- Optimal sampling (within this analysis): Importance sampling $p_i\propto L_i$ equalizes $\frac{p_i}{L_i}$ and yields the best bound within this framework.
- Step-size stability: Any $\eta\in(0,2)$ guarantees expected decrease per iteration. The optimal contraction factor in this bound is attained at $\eta=1$.
- Per-iteration cost: One partial derivative and one coordinate update; $O(1)$ arithmetic plus oracle access to $\nabla_i f$.
- Comparison to full GD: Full GD needs a full gradient per step and typically uses $L$-smoothness; RCGD-NS needs only coordinate smoothness and can be faster per iteration in high dimensions, achieving linear convergence under PL as well.

\section*{Discussion}
- Sensitivity to $L_i$: Normalizing by $L_i$ makes the update scale-invariant across coordinates; large $L_i$ induces smaller steps in that coordinate, ensuring descent.
- Effect of sampling: Under heterogeneous $L_i$, choosing $p_i\propto L_i$ eliminates the worst-coordinate bottleneck in the bound.
- Beyond PL: Without PL, one can still obtain expected descent and sublinear rates in convex settings; here we exploit PL to get linear convergence.

\section*{Preliminaries (Definitions and Lemmas)}
We collect basic facts used in the proof.

Lemma 1 (Coordinate-wise descent lemma).
Assume coordinate-wise smoothness with constant $L_i>0$ for coordinate $i$. Then for any $w\in\R^d$ and any $t\in\R$,
\[
f(w + t e_i) \;\le\; f(w) + t\,\nabla_i f(w) + \frac{L_i}{2}\,t^2.
\]
Proof.
Let $\varphi_i(s):=f(w+s e_i)$. Then $\varphi_i$ is differentiable with $\varphi_i'(s)=\nabla_i f(w+s e_i)$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\begin{align*}
f(w+te_i)-f(w) &= \int_{0}^{t} \varphi_i'(s)\,ds. \quad\text{[Step 1: integral representation of increment]}
\end{align*}
Add and subtract $\varphi_i'(0)=\nabla_i f(w)$ inside the integrand and use the triangle inequality:
\begin{align*}
\int_{0}^{t} \varphi_i'(s)\,ds &= \int_{0}^{t} \big(\varphi_i'(0) + (\varphi_i'(s)-\varphi_i'(0))\big)\,ds \\
&= t\,\varphi_i'(0) + \int_{0}^{t} \big(\varphi_i'(s)-\varphi_i'(0)\big)\,ds. \quad\text{[Step 2: linearity of integral]}
\end{align*}
By $L_i$-Lipschitz continuity of $\varphi_i'$,
\begin{align*}
\left|\int_{0}^{t} \big(\varphi_i'(s)-\varphi_i'(0)\big)\,ds\right| &\le \int_{0}^{|t|} \left|\varphi_i'(\operatorname{sign}(t)\,u)-\varphi_i'(0)\right|\,du \\
&\le \int_{0}^{|t|} L_i |u|\,du \;=\; \frac{L_i}{2}\,t^2. \quad\text{[Step 3: Lipschitz bound and integration]}
\end{align*}
Combining the inequalities and identifying $\varphi_i'(0)=\nabla_i f(w)$ gives the claim. \qed

Lemma 2 (PL inequality re-arranged).
If $f$ satisfies the PL condition with parameter $\mu>0$, then for all $w$,
\[
\|\nabla f(w)\|^2 \;\ge\; 2\mu\bigl(f(w)-f_\star\bigr).
\]
Proof. This is a restatement of the PL condition by multiplying both sides by 2. \qed

\section*{Main Proof (Step-by-Step Derivation)}
We prove the Main Convergence Theorem. Fix $t\ge 0$ and condition on $w_t$. Let $g_i := \nabla_i f(w_t)$ for brevity.

Given a sampled coordinate $i_t=i$, the update is $w_{t+1}=w_t + s_i e_i$ with
\[
s_i := -\eta\,\frac{g_i}{L_i}. \quad\text{[Step 4: definition of the coordinate step]}
\]
Apply Lemma 1 with $t=s_i$:
\begin{align*}
f(w_{t+1}) \;=\; f(w_t+s_i e_i) 
&\le f(w_t) + s_i\, g_i + \frac{L_i}{2}\, s_i^2. \quad\text{[Step 5: Lemma 1 applied at $(w_t,i)$]}
\end{align*}
Substitute $s_i$ from [Step 4] and expand:
\begin{align*}
s_i\, g_i &= -\eta\,\frac{g_i}{L_i}\,g_i \;=\; -\eta\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}, \quad\text{[Step 6: product expansion]}\\
\frac{L_i}{2} s_i^2 &= \frac{L_i}{2}\left(\eta^2\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i^2}\right)
\;=\; \frac{\eta^2}{2}\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}. \quad\text{[Step 7: square and simplify]}
\end{align*}
Hence
\begin{align*}
f(w_{t+1})
&\le f(w_t) -\eta\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i} + \frac{\eta^2}{2}\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i} \\
&= f(w_t) - \left(\eta - \frac{\eta^2}{2}\right)\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}
= f(w_t) - \frac{\eta(2-\eta)}{2}\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}. \quad\text{[Step 8: algebraic regrouping]}
\end{align*}
Take conditional expectation over $i_t\sim p$:
\begin{align*}
\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1}) \mid w_t\bigr]
&\le f(w_t) - \frac{\eta(2-\eta)}{2} \sum_{i=1}^d p_i\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}. \quad\text{[Step 9: linearity of expectation]}
\end{align*}
Lower bound the weighted sum by its smallest weight times the Euclidean norm squared:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^d p_i\,\frac{g_i^2}{L_i}
&\ge \left(\min_{i} \frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^d g_i^2
= \left(\min_{i} \frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2. \quad\text{[Step 10: monotonicity of sums with nonnegative terms]}
\end{align*}
Combine [Step 9] and [Step 10]:
\begin{align*}
\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1}) \mid w_t\bigr]
&\le f(w_t) - \frac{\eta(2-\eta)}{2}\left(\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2. \quad\text{[Step 11: substitution of the bound]}
\end{align*}
Subtract $f_\star$ on both sides and apply Lemma 2:
\begin{align*}
\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1})-f_\star \mid w_t\bigr]
&\le \bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr) - \frac{\eta(2-\eta)}{2}\left(\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2 \quad\text{[Step 12: re-centering at $f_\star$]}\\
&\le \bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr) - \eta(2-\eta)\left(\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\mu\,\bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr) \\
&= \left(1 - \eta(2-\eta)\,\mu\,\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}\right)\bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr), \quad\text{[Step 13: PL and algebra]}
\end{align*}
where in [Step 13] we used $\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2 \ge \mu\bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr)$.

Define $\rho:=\eta(2-\eta)\,\mu\,\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}\in(0,1]$ (the upper bound 1 is not needed but follows when parameters are normalized). Then
\begin{align*}
\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1})-f_\star \mid w_t\bigr] \;\le\; (1-\rho)\,\bigl(f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr). \quad\text{[Step 14: definition of $\rho$]}
\end{align*}
Finally, take total expectations and iterate via the tower property:
\begin{align*}
\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1})-f_\star\bigr]
&= \E\bigl[\;\E\bigl[f(w_{t+1})-f_\star \mid w_t\bigr]\;\bigr] \\
&\le (1-\rho)\,\E\bigl[f(w_t)-f_\star\bigr] \quad\text{[Step 15: tower property and [Step 14]]} \\
&\le \cdots \le (1-\rho)^{t+1}\,\bigl(f(w_0)-f_\star\bigr). \quad\text{[Step 16: induction]}
\end{align*}
This proves the theorem (relabel $t+1\mapsto t$).

\section*{Rate Derivation (With Constants)}
From the proof, the contraction factor per iteration is
\[
1-\rho, \quad \rho=\eta(2-\eta)\,\mu\,\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}.
\]
- Optimal $\eta$ within $(0,2)$ for this bound is $\eta^\star=1$, yielding $\rho^\star=\mu\,\min_{i}\frac{p_i}{L_i}$.
- Importance sampling $p_i=\frac{L_i}{\sum_j L_j}$ makes $\frac{p_i}{L_i}=\frac{1}{\sum_j L_j}$ constant across $i$, so $\rho^\star=\frac{\mu}{\sum_{j=1}^d L_j}$.
- Uniform sampling $p_i=\frac{1}{d}$ yields $\rho^\star=\frac{\mu}{d\max_i L_i}$.

Iteration complexity to reach $\E[f(w_T)-f_\star]\le \varepsilon$:
\[
T \;\ge\; \frac{1}{\rho}\,\log\!\left(\frac{f(w_0)-f_\star}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]

\section*{Special Cases}
- Quadratic objectives: For $f(w)=\tfrac{1}{2}w^\top Q w - b^\top w + c$ with $Q\succeq 0$, PL holds with $\mu=\lambda_{\min}^+(Q)$ (smallest positive eigenvalue), and $L_i=Q_{ii}$ suffices when $Q$ is diagonally dominant; the above rate specializes accordingly.
- Strongly convex $L$-smooth: If $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex and has coordinate-wise constants $L_i$, then PL holds with the same $\mu$, and the result gives linear convergence as above.
